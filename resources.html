Free study resources

Practical guides to help you improve your English, build vocabulary, strengthen your grammar and develop better typing skills.

1. Study habits that actually work

You do not need to study for hours every day to make progress. A consistent routine and the right learning methods can help you remember more.

Use active recall

After reading a topic, close your notes and try to explain what you learned without looking. This helps you discover what you understand and what you need to review.

Review at intervals

Instead of studying something once and forgetting it, revisit it after a day, a few days and a week. Short review sessions can help strengthen your memory.

Learn words in sentences

Do not memorize a new word alone. Learn its meaning, see how it is used in a sentence and create your own example.

Keep an error log

Write down mistakes you make in grammar, vocabulary or typing. Record the correct answer and review your list regularly. Your mistakes can become a useful personal study guide.

2. Grammar quick reference

These simple grammar rules can help you write clearer, more accurate English sentences.

For vs. since

Use for to describe a period of time. Use since to identify when something started.

  • I have studied English for three years.
  • I have studied English since 2023.

Fewer vs. less

Use fewer with countable plural nouns and less with uncountable nouns.

  • There are fewer students in class today.
  • I have less time to study today.

Prepositions of time and place

These common prepositions can be confusing. Use this table as a quick guide.

PrepositionCommon useExample
AtSpecific times or pointsThe lesson starts at 9 a.m.
OnDays and datesWe have a test on Monday.
InMonths, years and longer periodsShe graduated in 2025.
ByA deadline or latest timePlease submit it by Friday.

Subject-verb agreement

The verb must agree with its subject. Pay attention to these common examples.

  • Each student is responsible for their work.
  • The news is encouraging.
  • Neither answer is correct.
  • Either the teacher or the students are presenting first.

Remember that words such as information and advice are generally uncountable in English. Say "some advice," not "an advice."

3. Common English mistakes to avoid

Some expressions sound familiar but are grammatically incorrect. Use these examples to improve your everyday writing and speaking.

IncorrectCorrectWhy?
She don't like it.She doesn't like it.Use "doesn't" with he, she and it.
I am having 21 years.I am 21 years old.English uses "be" to state age.
We discussed about the issue.We discussed the issue."Discuss" does not need "about" before its object.
She is more taller.She is taller.Do not normally use "more" with an -er comparative.
I look forward to meet you.I look forward to meeting you.Use a noun or -ing form after "look forward to."
He is one of my friend.He is one of my friends.Use a plural noun after "one of my."
She explained me the topic.She explained the topic to me.Use "explain something to someone."

Make the corrections part of your routine

When you notice a mistake in your writing, do not only correct it. Learn the rule behind the correction and create another sentence using that rule.

Some English words sound alike or have similar meanings but are used differently. Understanding these differences will help you avoid mistakes.

Practice tip: Choose three pairs each day. Write one original sentence for each word and check whether you have used it correctly.

5. How to build a stronger vocabulary

A strong vocabulary helps you understand what you read, express your ideas clearly and communicate with confidence. You do not need to memorize hundreds of words at once.

Learn words in context

Read the sentence around a new word to understand how it is used. For example: "The instructions were concise, so everyone understood them quickly." Here, concise means brief but clear.

Learn related words together

Word families help you understand how words change according to their use.

  • Decide: verb — I need to decide today.
  • Decision: noun — It was a difficult decision.
  • Decisive: adjective — She took decisive action.

Use new words in your own sentences

After learning a word, write a sentence connected to your daily life. Using a word yourself can help you remember its meaning and appropriate use.

Group words by topic

Learn related words together, such as words about education, health, work, communication or technology. This makes it easier to connect new words with ideas you already understand.

Review regularly

Keep a vocabulary notebook with the word, its meaning, an example sentence and any useful synonyms or opposites. Review previous entries before adding too many new words.

6. Improve your typing speed and accuracy

Typing well is not only about speed. Accuracy, comfort and consistency are equally important, especially when studying, working or writing online.

Learn the home-row keys

On a standard QWERTY keyboard, place your left fingers on A, S, D and F and your right fingers on J, K, L and the semicolon key. Let your thumbs rest near the spacebar.

Prioritize accuracy before speed

Start at a comfortable pace and focus on pressing the correct keys. As your accuracy improves, your typing speed can increase naturally.

Look at the screen

Try to reduce how often you look down at the keyboard. Learning key positions gradually helps you type more confidently.

Maintain a comfortable posture

Sit comfortably, relax your shoulders and keep your wrists in a neutral position. Take breaks during longer practice sessions.

Track your progress

Practise regularly and record your words per minute (WPM) and accuracy. Compare results over time rather than worrying about every individual test.

7. A simple 15-minute daily English routine

You can improve your English without spending hours studying every day. Try this simple routine and adjust it to fit your schedule.

Minutes 1–5: Build your vocabulary

Learn two or three new words. Read their meanings, study example sentences and create your own sentences using each word.

Minutes 6–10: Practise grammar

Choose one grammar rule, such as subject-verb agreement or prepositions. Read the rule and write two correct sentences that demonstrate it.

Minutes 11–15: Read and write

Read a short paragraph and summarize its main idea in your own words. Check your spelling, punctuation and sentence structure.

Make the routine consistent

Practising for 15 minutes regularly can help you build a learning habit. Review what you learned at the end of each week and spend extra time on areas you find difficult.

8. How to write clearer English sentences

Clear writing helps readers understand your message without unnecessary effort. Whether you are writing an email, an assignment or a social media caption, these techniques can help.

Make sure every sentence expresses a complete thought

A complete sentence usually contains a subject and a verb and expresses a complete idea.

  • Incomplete: Because I was tired.
  • Complete: I went to bed early because I was tired.

Keep sentences focused

Try to communicate one main idea at a time. If a sentence contains too many unrelated points, divide it into shorter sentences.

Use punctuation correctly

Periods mark the end of statements, commas separate certain sentence elements, and question marks end direct questions. Punctuation helps readers understand how your ideas fit together.

Choose precise words

Instead of using vague expressions, choose words that communicate exactly what you mean. For example, "The meeting lasted two hours" is more precise than "The meeting lasted a long time."

Proofread before submitting

After writing, read your work slowly. Check for spelling errors, repeated words, missing punctuation and sentences that are difficult to understand. Reading aloud can help you notice awkward phrasing.
