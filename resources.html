Free study resources

Short, practical resources to help you improve your English, vocabulary, typing and study habits.

Study habits that actually work

  • Short and regular beats long and rare. Fifteen minutes a day builds far more than two hours once a month.
  • Test yourself. Reading a rule feels productive; recalling it without looking is what makes it stick.
  • Space your review. Look at a rule again after one day, then three, then a week.
  • Learn in sentences, not lists. A word you met in a sentence is easier to use than a word from a list.
  • Keep an error log. Write down what you got wrong and why. The same mistakes repeat until you write them down.

Grammar quick reference

A few rules that come up again and again in the tests.

for vs since

for + a length of time (for two hours, for six years). since + a starting point (since 2015, since Monday).

fewer vs less

fewer with countable nouns (fewer chairs, fewer mistakes). less with uncountable nouns (less water, less time).

Subject-verb agreement traps

  • Words like news, information, advice look plural but are singular: the news is good.
  • Each / every one / neither take a singular verb: each of them has.
  • With either … or / neither … nor, the verb agrees with the subject nearest to it: neither Tom nor his brothers are coming.

Commonly confused words

WordsMeaningExample
affect / effectaffect = verb, to influence · effect = noun, a result The rain affected the match. It had no effect on the score.
its / it'sits = belonging to it · it's = it is The dog wagged its tail. It's raining.
your / you'reyour = belonging to you · you're = you are Is this your book? You're early.
there / their / they'replace · belonging to them · they are Put it there. Their bags are ready. They're here.
then / thanthen = at that time · than = comparison Finish this, then rest. Taller than me.
rise / raiserise = go up on its own · raise = lift something Prices rose. They raised prices.
accept / exceptaccept = receive · except = not including I accept your offer. Everyone except me.

Building vocabulary

  • Learn word families. From rely you get reliable, reliably, reliability and reliance — five words for the price of one.
  • Meet words in context. Note the sentence you found the word in, not just the definition.
  • Use each new word within a day. Write a sentence, say it aloud, or message a friend with it.
  • Group by topic. Ten words about work, ten about food, ten about travel are easier to recall than thirty random ones.

Typing technique

  • Home row first. Rest your fingers on A S D F and J K L ; and return there after every key.
  • Accuracy before speed. Speed follows accuracy, not the other way round. Aim for zero errors at a comfortable pace, then push the pace.
  • Sit upright, wrists neutral. Keep your elbows close to your body and your wrists straight rather than bent upwards.
  • Look at the screen. Train by touch — it feels slower for a week and then it is much faster.
  • Measure, don't guess. Take the typing test once a week and compare numbers, not feelings.
